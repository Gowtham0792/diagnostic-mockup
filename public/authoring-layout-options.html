Authoring tool layout — trend-grounded options v3

Went and checked what's actually current before proposing anything this time, rather than guessing. The consistent theme across 2026 UI-trend coverage is restraint — glassmorphism, bento grids and dark mode are all still current, but the flashy, gradient-heavy, glowing version of them is already reading as dated/AI-generated. What's replacing it: calm minimalism, purposeful (not decorative) motion, and bento/progressive-disclosure patterns that reveal detail on interaction instead of showing everything at once.

What changed vs. last time: dropped the gradient-mesh glow and floating glass-everywhere look. These four lean on typography, whitespace and restrained motion instead of visual effects to feel current.

1 · Calm editorial minimalism

No borders-as-decoration, no shadows, no color except one accent used once. A thin progress hairline, a small uppercase eyebrow, then a large confident heading. This is the "quiet luxury" register — closest to how Linear's or Arc's own marketing pages actually look today.

Grounded in: 2026 coverage naming minimalist/calm design and expressive typography as the dominant SaaS direction.

2 · Progressive wizard, practical

A thin top progress line, a plain-text breadcrumb of steps (current bold), and a "Save & exit" link — the pattern behind Amazon's checkout and most high-completion-rate wizards. Tiles fade/slide in on entry, restrained rather than showy.

Grounded in: progressive-disclosure wizard best practice — step numbering + persistent progress + an escape hatch reduce drop-off.

3 · Active bento interactive — hover it

A bento grid, but calm: neutral soft shadows, no color wash. The trend isn't just the asymmetric grid — it's that tiles now reveal something on interaction instead of sitting static. Hover a function tile below; a one-line peek slides up.

Grounded in: 2026 coverage describing bento grids "going active" — hover reveals an extra layer instead of just a color change.

4 · Dark mode, restrained identity

Flat near-black, not a gradient wash — 1px hairline borders, one accent color spent only on the active state and progress fill. No blur, no glow. Dark as a considered default, not a toggle bolted on.

Grounded in: 2026 coverage on dark mode becoming a full visual identity for tech/fintech tools, contrasted with the "everything glows" version that's already fading.
